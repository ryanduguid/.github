# Code of conduct

These projects are maintained by one person and are used in a professional
accounting context. The standard here is the one that applies in a client
meeting: disagree about the work, not about the person.

## Expected

- Keep discussion on the code, the data, the legislation or the workflow.
- Assume the other person is reading the same source you are and reached a
  different conclusion honestly. Cite the section, the commit or the line.
- Accept a correction without prosecuting it, and give one the same way.
- Respect the data boundary. Do not post client, taxpayer, employee or payroll
  data in an issue, a pull request, a commit or a discussion. Fabricate a
  minimal reproduction instead.

## Not accepted

- Personal attacks, harassment, or demeaning comments about a person or a group.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwanted personal attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately posting real client, taxpayer or payroll data belonging to
  someone else.
- Sustained disruption: repeatedly reopening a settled decision, or arguing a
  point already answered with a source.

## Professional-advice boundary

Nothing in these repositories is tax, accounting, legal or financial advice, and
no issue or pull request thread creates a professional engagement. Requests for
personal advice on a real tax position will be declined, not because the
question is unwelcome, but because answering it responsibly requires an engaged
adviser with the full facts.

## Scope

This applies in every project space (issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
comments, releases) and whenever someone is representing a project in public.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately to the maintainer through the contact link on
[github.com/ryanduguid](https://github.com/ryanduguid). Reports are read by the
maintainer alone and are not shared further without the reporter's agreement,
except where disclosure is legally required.

If a report concerns the maintainer, or the maintainer does not act, escalate to
GitHub through its own reporting and abuse channels, which can act on accounts
and content independently of any project.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and history, a response may be a private correction, a
public correction with the offending content edited or removed, a temporary
block from interaction, or a permanent block. Deliberate exposure of another
person's confidential client data is treated as the most serious category and
skips the graduated path.
